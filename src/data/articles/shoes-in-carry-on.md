Shoes are a packing puzzle for most travelers — they're bulky, they're heavy, but sometimes you really need an extra pair. Whether you're wondering if your high heels can go in your carry-on or how to fit shoes into your personal item, TSA's position is straightforward. Here's everything you need to know.

---

## TSA Rules Summary

- **Shoes in Carry-On:** ✅ Allowed — No TSA restrictions
- **Shoes in Personal Item:** ✅ Allowed — No TSA restrictions
- **Shoes in Checked Baggage:** ✅ Allowed
- **Wearing Shoes Through Security:** ✅ Required for most travelers (may need removal)
- **TSA Declaration Required:** No

---

## Official Regulations

### TSA Has No Restrictions on Shoes in Carry-On

The Transportation Security Administration does not prohibit or restrict shoes in carry-on bags or personal items. You can pack as many pairs of shoes as fit in your bag without any TSA concern.

However, **shoes worn through the security checkpoint** may need to be removed and sent through the X-ray machine separately:

- **Standard screening lanes:** Shoes must be removed and placed in a bin for X-ray
- **TSA PreCheck lanes:** No shoe removal required (one of the key PreCheck benefits)
- **Children under 12:** Do not need to remove shoes
- **Adults over 75:** May keep shoes on (subject to officer discretion)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The shoe screening requirement exists because of the 2001 "shoe bomber" incident, where Richard Reid concealed explosives in his shoe. This led to universal shoe removal at US airport security — a policy that remains in place today.

### Cleats and Spiked Shoes

Soccer cleats, baseball cleats, and golf shoes with metal spikes or studs are a gray area:

- TSA does not have an explicit policy banning sports cleats from carry-on
- However, officers may determine that long metal cleats could be used as a weapon
- **Best practice:** Pack sports cleats with spikes/studs in your checked bag to avoid any discussion at the checkpoint

## How to Pack Shoes in Your Carry-On

Shoes are notoriously space-inefficient. Here are the best strategies:

### Strategy 1: Shoe Bags
Pack each pair in a reusable shoe bag or shower cap to keep dirty soles away from your clothes. Many packing cubes have dedicated shoe compartments.

### Strategy 2: Stuff the Shoes
Roll socks and underwear and stuff them inside shoes to maximize space efficiency. This is one of the oldest travel packing tricks.

### Strategy 3: Wear the Bulkiest Pair
If you're bringing boots or thick-soled shoes, wear them on the plane. Shoes on your feet don't count against your carry-on or personal item allowance.

### Strategy 4: Minimize Shoe Count
For most trips, 2 pairs maximum is the goal: one comfortable walking pair (worn on the plane) and one versatile pair for evenings or formal occasions.

## Personal Item vs. Carry-On for Shoes

If you're flying with a personal item only (budget airlines, trying to avoid fees):

- Standard personal item dimensions are approximately 18 x 14 x 8 inches
- One pair of regular shoes can typically fit alongside a few other items
- High heels or bulky shoes may struggle in small personal items
- Roll-top shoes like sandals and flip flops pack much more efficiently

## High Heels in Carry-On

High heels are specifically allowed in carry-on bags. TSA does not restrict heels based on heel height, material, or style. Whether you have 2-inch block heels or 5-inch stilettos — both are permitted in your carry-on.

Practical tips for heels in carry-on:
- Pack heels heel-to-toe to save space
- Use shoe bags to protect heel tips from snagging on other items
- Place heels sole-to-sole (or sole against a flat surface) to protect clothing from dirt

## International Travel with Shoes

Shoes are not subject to customs restrictions in any major destination country for personal use quantities (typically 1–3 pairs for personal travel). You do not need to declare shoes at customs unless they are new items exceeding your duty-free allowance.

## Essential Traveler Tips

1. **Wear your heaviest shoes** — Boots and thick-soled shoes are heavy and bulky. Wearing them on the plane eliminates the weight and space problem entirely.
2. **Use packing cubes** — Shoes compartmentalize beautifully in packing cubes, keeping them separate from clothes.
3. **2-pair rule** — Most experienced travelers follow the 2-pair rule: 1 pair on your feet, 1 pair in the bag. It covers nearly any trip.
4. **PreCheck for shoe convenience** — TSA PreCheck members don't remove shoes at security, making shoe choice less relevant at the checkpoint.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Mistake 1: Forgetting to remove shoes at standard security.** In standard (non-PreCheck) lanes, shoes must come off. Wearing slip-on shoes makes this significantly faster.
- **Mistake 2: Packing shoes loose with clothes.** Dirty shoe soles will transfer to your clothes. Always use shoe bags.
- **Mistake 3: Packing sports cleats in carry-on.** The metal studs/spikes on cleats can trigger officer discretion. Play it safe and check them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can you put shoes in your carry-on?**
A: Yes. TSA has no restrictions on shoes in carry-on bags. You can bring as many pairs as fit.

**Q: Are shoes allowed in carry-on luggage?**
A: Yes. All types of shoes — sneakers, heels, boots, sandals — are permitted in carry-on bags.

**Q: Can I put heels in my carry-on?**
A: Yes. High heels are fully permitted in carry-on luggage. There is no TSA restriction on heel height or type.

**Q: Can I bring shoes in my personal item bag?**
A: Yes. Shoes fit in personal item bags (subject to the airline's size restrictions for personal items).

**Q: Do I have to take my shoes off at airport security?**
A: In standard TSA lanes, yes — shoes must be removed. TSA PreCheck members do not need to remove shoes.

**Q: Can you wear heels on a plane?**
A: Yes. You can wear heels through security (shoes must be removed in standard lanes) and on the plane.

---

## Conclusion

Packing shoes in your carry-on is completely TSA-approved — no restrictions on type, quantity, or style. The only shoe-related rule is removing them at the security checkpoint (unless you have TSA PreCheck). Pack smart by wearing your bulkiest pair, stuffing socks inside shoes to save space, and using shoe bags to protect your clothes. Two pairs for most trips is the sweet spot.
