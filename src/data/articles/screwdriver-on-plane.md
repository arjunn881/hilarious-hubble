Whether you're a tradesperson, a hobbyist, or someone fixing up a rental on arrival, knowing whether you can bring a screwdriver on a plane is essential. TSA has a clear rule based on screwdriver length — and it's more nuanced than most travelers realize. Here's the complete breakdown.

---

## TSA Rules Summary

- **Screwdrivers (7 inches or shorter): Carry-On:** ✅ Allowed
- **Screwdrivers (over 7 inches): Carry-On:** ❌ Not Allowed
- **All Screwdrivers in Checked Baggage:** ✅ Allowed
- **Multi-Tools with Screwdriver: Carry-On:** ❌ Not Allowed (bladed multi-tools prohibited)
- **TSA Declaration Required:** No

---

## Official Regulations

### The 7-Inch Rule for Screwdrivers

The TSA has a specific rule for screwdrivers: screwdrivers **7 inches or shorter in total length** are permitted in carry-on baggage. Screwdrivers longer than 7 inches must go in checked luggage.

This 7-inch measurement is the total length of the tool — handle plus shaft — not just the blade length.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Measure your screwdriver before packing. A standard household Phillips-head screwdriver is typically 8–10 inches total — longer than the 7-inch carry-on limit. Most pocket/precision screwdrivers (like Wiha, Klein, or iFixit precision sets) fall under 7 inches.

### What Counts as Under 7 Inches?

Common screwdrivers that typically pass:
- **Precision screwdrivers** (electronics repair sets) — Usually 4–6 inches total ✅
- **Pocket screwdrivers** — Usually 4–6 inches ✅
- **iFixit repair kits** — Most handle/bit combos under 7 inches ✅

Common screwdrivers that typically do NOT pass:
- **Standard household screwdrivers** — Usually 8–12 inches total ❌
- **Long shaft screwdrivers** — ❌
- **Impact drivers** — ❌ (tool head classification applies)

### Multi-Tools with Screwdrivers

Multi-tools (Leatherman, Gerber, Swiss Army) that include a screwdriver attachment are **not allowed in carry-on** if they contain any blade (knife, saw, or cutting tool). Even if you only want the screwdriver, a bladed multi-tool is fully prohibited from the cabin.

Multi-tools without blades (screwdriver-only multi-tools) may be allowed if the total length is under 7 inches — but these are rare.

**Bottom line for multi-tools: Put them in checked baggage.**

---

## Screwdrivers in Checked Baggage

All screwdrivers — regardless of length, type, or size — are permitted in checked baggage. There are no restrictions on tools in checked luggage as long as they are not flammable or otherwise hazardous.

For safety, wrap sharp screwdriver tips in tape or a protective cap to prevent injury to baggage handlers.

## International Rules for Screwdrivers

Most international aviation authorities follow similar logic to TSA:
- Short precision/pocket screwdrivers: generally permitted in carry-on
- Standard household screwdrivers: checked bags only
- Multi-tools with blades: carry-on prohibited

**UK / EU (EASA):** Sharp tools over 6cm (2.4 inches) in the blade are prohibited from the cabin. Note this refers to the blade, not total tool length — verify with your specific departure airport.

**Australia (CASA):** Tools with sharp points or cutting edges over 6cm blade length are prohibited in carry-on.

## Essential Traveler Tips

1. **Measure before you pack** — Run a tape measure across your screwdriver (handle to tip). If it's over 7 inches, check it.
2. **Precision kits for tech travelers** — If you need a screwdriver for laptop or device repairs on the road, an iFixit or Wiha precision kit stays under 7 inches and is carry-on approved.
3. **Ship tools for job sites** — For contractors flying to job sites, shipping your toolbox via UPS or FedEx ground is often cheaper than overweight checked bag fees.
4. **Buy disposable tools at the destination** — Basic screwdrivers are available at any hardware store for a few dollars. Buy on arrival and leave behind.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Mistake 1: Assuming a household screwdriver is under 7 inches.** Most standard screwdrivers sold in hardware stores are 8–12 inches total. Always measure.
- **Mistake 2: Packing a multi-tool with a blade in carry-on.** Even if you only need the screwdriver attachment, any bladed multi-tool is banned from the cabin.
- **Mistake 3: Forgetting a screwdriver left in a work bag from a previous job.** Always check all compartments of your work backpack or tool bag before heading to the airport.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can you bring a screwdriver on a plane?**
A: Yes — if it's 7 inches or shorter in total length, it's allowed in carry-on. Longer screwdrivers must go in checked baggage.

**Q: Can you take a screwdriver on a plane in carry-on?**
A: Only if the total length (handle + shaft) is 7 inches or less.

**Q: Can you bring a screwdriver in checked luggage?**
A: Yes. All screwdrivers are allowed in checked baggage with no length restrictions.

**Q: Can you bring a multi-tool on a plane?**
A: Bladed multi-tools (with a knife or cutting edge) are not allowed in carry-on. They must go in checked bags. Multi-tools without blades may be allowed in carry-on if under 7 inches.

**Q: Can you take tools on a plane?**
A: Short hand tools (under 7 inches) are generally carry-on approved. All tools are permitted in checked baggage.

## Conclusion

Screwdrivers follow a straightforward 7-inch rule: under 7 inches = carry-on OK, over 7 inches = checked bags only. For most travelers, a standard household screwdriver is too long for carry-on, but a precision or pocket screwdriver makes the cut. Always measure before packing, keep multi-tools in checked luggage, and consider shipping heavy tool loads ahead for job-site trips.
