**Global Entry vs TSA PreCheck** is one of the most common questions travelers ask when looking to speed up airport security. Both programs offer expedited screening benefits — but they serve different types of travelers. This guide breaks down the differences, costs, and which one you should get.

## What Is TSA PreCheck?

**TSA PreCheck** is a U.S. government trusted traveler program that gives approved members access to expedited security lanes at 200+ U.S. airports (learn more in our standalone guide on [What Is TSA PreCheck?](/guide/what-is-tsa-precheck)). In the PreCheck lane, you do **not** need to:

- Remove shoes
- Remove laptops from bags
- Remove your quart-sized [3-1-1 liquids bag](/guide/tsa-311-liquids-rule)
- Remove belts or light jackets

**Cost:** $78 for 5 years ($15.60/year)

TSA PreCheck is purely a **domestic departure** benefit — it only applies at U.S. airport security checkpoints when you are flying out. It does nothing for you at customs, immigration, or when you arrive back in the U.S. from an international trip.

---

## What Is Global Entry?

**Global Entry** is a U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) program that gives approved travelers expedited entry into the United States when returning from international travel. Global Entry members:

- Use **Global Entry kiosks** in the arrivals hall (faster than regular customs lines)
- Skip the standard customs and immigration officer line
- Complete customs declarations digitally at a kiosk
- **Automatically receive TSA PreCheck** as a benefit

**Cost:** $120 for 5 years ($24/year)

Global Entry is the better deal for **international travelers** because it includes TSA PreCheck AND adds the fast customs processing when returning to the U.S.

## TSA PreCheck vs Global Entry: Key Differences

### 1. Price: $42 More for Global Entry

| Program | Cost | Per Year |
|---|---|---|
| TSA PreCheck | $78 | $15.60 |
| Global Entry | $120 | $24.00 |
| **Difference** | **$42** | **$8.40** |

For $42 more over 5 years — less than $1/month extra — Global Entry gives you TSA PreCheck **plus** expedited customs on every international return. It's almost always the better value.

### 2. Application Process

**TSA PreCheck:**
1. Apply online at tsa.gov
2. Schedule in-person fingerprinting appointment (IDEMIA or Telos enrollment centers)
3. Attend appointment (~15 min)
4. Receive KTN in 3–5 days

**Global Entry:**
1. Apply online at cbp.gov/Trusted-Traveler-Programs
2. Application reviewed by CBP (can take weeks to months)
3. Receive "conditional approval"
4. Schedule in-person interview at a Global Entry Enrollment Center (airports, CBP offices)
5. Complete interview (~10–15 min)
6. Full approval — receive PASSID (which is also your TSA PreCheck KTN)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Global Entry interviews are less immediately available than TSA PreCheck enrollment appointments. At busy airports, interview slots can be booked out 1–3 months. Apply early.

### 3. Background Check Depth

Both programs run criminal background checks, but Global Entry's is more thorough — it includes international criminal history verification since the program covers international travel and customs entry.

### 4. Conditional Interview vs Enrollment Appointment

- **TSA PreCheck:** You go to an enrollment center for fingerprinting. No formal "interview" — it's mostly paperwork and biometrics.
- **Global Entry:** You attend a formal interview with a CBP officer who reviews your travel history, asks questions about past international trips, and verifies your documents.

## Which Should You Get?

### Get TSA PreCheck If:
- ✅ You fly **domestically only** (or rarely internationally)
- ✅ You want the fastest approval (days vs. months)
- ✅ You want the lowest cost ($78 vs $120)
- ✅ You fly 3+ domestic trips per year and want faster security

### Get Global Entry If:
- ✅ You fly **internationally** even once a year
- ✅ You want TSA PreCheck **included** at no extra meaningful cost ($42 more total)
- ✅ You frequently return to the U.S. from abroad and hate customs lines
- ✅ You travel for business internationally

> [!TIP]
> **For most travelers who fly at all internationally: choose Global Entry.** The $42 premium over 5 years is negligible, and you get the full benefit of both programs in one.

## Does Global Entry Include TSA PreCheck?

**Yes — Global Entry automatically includes TSA PreCheck.** When you are approved for Global Entry, CBP issues you a PASSID number. This same number functions as your TSA Known Traveler Number (KTN). You add it to your airline profile and bookings, and you'll see "TSA PRE✓" on your boarding pass — exactly like a standalone TSA PreCheck member.

There is **no extra step** to activate TSA PreCheck with Global Entry. It's automatic.

## How to Apply for Global Entry

1. **Create an account** at [cbp.gov Trusted Traveler Programs](https://ttp.cbp.dhs.gov/)
2. **Complete the application** — personal info, travel history, employment
3. **Pay the $120 fee** (non-refundable even if denied)
4. **Wait for conditional approval** — CBP reviews your background (days to weeks)
5. **Schedule your interview** at a Global Entry Enrollment Center (locations at major airports and CBP offices)
6. **Attend your interview** — bring passport + one more government ID
7. **Receive approval** — your PASSID is emailed; use it as your TSA KTN

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is Global Entry worth it compared to TSA PreCheck?**
A: Yes, for international travelers. For $42 more over 5 years, you get TSA PreCheck included plus expedited customs when returning to the U.S. from abroad.

**Q: Does Global Entry automatically give you TSA PreCheck?**
A: Yes. Global Entry approval automatically includes TSA PreCheck. Your PASSID doubles as a TSA Known Traveler Number.

**Q: Which is faster to get — Global Entry or TSA PreCheck?**
A: TSA PreCheck is much faster. You can have your KTN in 3–5 days. Global Entry requires a CBP interview that can take weeks to months to schedule.

**Q: Can I have both Global Entry and TSA PreCheck?**
A: You don't need both — Global Entry includes TSA PreCheck. Having both separately would be redundant.

**Q: Is NEXUS better than Global Entry?**
A: NEXUS ($50/5 years) includes TSA PreCheck and fast U.S.-Canada border crossing. If you travel to Canada frequently, NEXUS is the best value. It doesn't cover other international customs entries the way Global Entry does.

**Q: How do I use Global Entry when I return from an international flight?**
A: When you land at a U.S. airport with Global Entry kiosks, follow signs for "Global Entry / Automated Passport Control." Scan your passport, complete the customs declaration on the kiosk screen, and proceed through without waiting in the standard customs line.

## Conclusion

For purely domestic travelers: **TSA PreCheck ($78)** is sufficient and fast to get. For anyone who travels internationally even occasionally: **Global Entry ($120)** is almost always the smarter choice — it includes TSA PreCheck and adds fast customs processing for only $42 more over 5 years. If your credit card covers the fee, get Global Entry. Full stop.
