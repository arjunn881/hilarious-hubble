Extension cords seem harmless enough — but there's been increasing TSA scrutiny of power-related items in carry-on bags. Whether you're bringing a power strip for a conference, a surge protector for a hotel stay, or a simple extension cord for a rental, here's what TSA actually allows.

---

## TSA Rules Summary

- **Extension Cords in Carry-On:** ✅ Allowed
- **Extension Cords in Checked Baggage:** ✅ Allowed
- **Power Strips in Carry-On:** ✅ Generally Allowed (without surge protection chemicals)
- **Surge Protectors in Carry-On:** ✅ Allowed
- **TSA Declaration Required:** No

---

## Official Regulations

### Extension Cords Are Permitted

The TSA explicitly permits extension cords in both carry-on and checked baggage. Extension cords are not classified as hazardous materials and pose no security threat at the checkpoint.

Standard household extension cords — single-outlet, multi-outlet, retractable, or flat-cord varieties — all pass without issue.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> While TSA permits extension cords, **individual airlines and international aviation authorities may have restrictions**. Some airlines prohibit power strips with surge protection circuits in checked luggage due to internal electrical components.

### Power Strips and Surge Protectors

This is where it gets nuanced:

- **Simple power strips** (just multiple outlets, no surge protection circuitry) — ✅ Allowed in carry-on and checked bags
- **Surge protectors** (with internal circuit breaker or MOV components) — ✅ Allowed in carry-on; some airlines restrict in checked baggage
- **Power strips with USB charging ports** — ✅ Allowed in carry-on; check airline rules for checked bags

The FAA's concern with surge protectors in checked baggage relates to their internal components, which in rare cases can overheat. Most airlines default to requiring surge protectors in carry-on rather than checked luggage.

### What About Smart Plugs and Travel Adapters?

- **Smart plugs** (WiFi-enabled plugs) — ✅ Allowed in carry-on and checked
- **Universal travel adapters** — ✅ Allowed in carry-on and checked
- **Voltage converters** — ✅ Allowed, but verify with your airline for larger converters

## International Considerations

Extension cords work fine for air travel internationally, but remember:

- **Plug type differences** — Your US extension cord won't plug into UK (Type G), EU (Type C/F), or Australian (Type I) outlets without an adapter
- **Voltage differences** — The US uses 120V/60Hz. Most of the world uses 220–240V/50Hz. Plugging a US device into a 240V outlet without a voltage converter can damage or destroy it
- **Bring a universal adapter** — Pair your extension cord with a universal travel adapter for international use

## Essential Traveler Tips

1. **Coil it neatly in your bag** — A tangled extension cord looks suspicious on X-ray. Coil it and secure with a velcro tie before packing.
2. **Carry power strips in your carry-on** — Even if the airline doesn't explicitly prohibit them in checked bags, power strips are better off in carry-on where they're not buried in luggage handlers' equipment.
3. **Check hotel policies** — Some hotel rooms have limited outlets. A 6-outlet power strip is one of the most useful items a business traveler can carry.
4. **Use a compact travel power strip** — Products like the BESTEK travel power strip or Epicka universal adapter combine surge protection, USB ports, and a short cord in a single compact unit — perfect for carry-on.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Mistake 1: Packing a power strip loosely in a checked bag.** The cord can tangle with other items and the strip can be damaged. Pack it in a small bag or organizer pouch.
- **Mistake 2: Bringing a US extension cord internationally without a voltage check.** If the device you're powering requires 120V and you plug it into a 240V outlet via the cord, you risk damaging electronics.
- **Mistake 3: Assuming any power strip is fine in checked bags on all airlines.** Some airlines (especially budget European carriers) have explicit restrictions on electrical items with internal components in checked luggage. Check with your airline.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can you bring an extension cord on a plane?**
A: Yes. Extension cords are allowed in both carry-on and checked baggage by the TSA.

**Q: Can you bring a power strip on a plane?**
A: Yes. Basic power strips are allowed in carry-on and checked bags. Surge protectors are best packed in carry-on.

**Q: Can you bring a surge protector on a plane?**
A: Generally yes in carry-on. Some airlines restrict surge protectors in checked baggage — verify with your airline.

**Q: Can I pack an extension cord in my checked luggage?**
A: Yes. Extension cords are permitted in checked baggage.

**Q: Can you fly with an extension cord internationally?**
A: Yes, but bring a voltage adapter if traveling between 120V (US) and 240V (most of the world) countries.

---

## Conclusion

Extension cords and power strips are travel-friendly and fully TSA-approved. For domestic US travel, pack them wherever convenient — carry-on or checked. For international travel, remember to bring a plug adapter and check voltage compatibility. When in doubt, keep power strips with surge protection in your carry-on rather than checked bags to satisfy the strictest airline policies.
