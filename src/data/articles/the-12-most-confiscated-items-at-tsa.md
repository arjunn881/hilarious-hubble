# 12 Everyday Items TSA Confiscates Every Day

Every single day, hundreds of thousands of otherwise law-abiding travelers watch in shock as TSA checkpoint officers seize belongings they were positive were permitted in carry-on baggage.

Airport surrender bins overflow not just with oversized water bottles, but with expensive culinary gear, childhood souvenirs, camping equipment, and luxury toiletries. The confusion stems from a simple disconnect: what makes sense in everyday civilian life does not align with federal aviation security codes under **Title 49 CFR Part 1540**.

Here are the 12 most frequently confiscated items at airport checkpoints, why officers take them, and how you can save your valuables before your next flight.

---

## The 12 Most Confiscated Items at Checkpoints

### 1. Souvenir Snow Globes
Souvenirs from gift shops are the #1 holiday heartbreak at airport checkpoints. 
* **The Rule:** A standard souvenir snow globe contains liquid. Unless the entire globe is smaller than a tennis ball (containing under 3.4 ounces / 100 ml of liquid total), it is **prohibited in carry-on**.
* **The Solution:** Always wrap snow globes in protective clothing and pack them in your **checked bag**.

### 2. [Peanut Butter](/items/peanut-butter/), Nutella & Gourmet Jams
As detailed in federal security advisories, spreads that conform to their containers are classified as gels and liquids.
* **The Rule:** Any jar exceeding 3.4 oz (100 ml) must be surrendered, even if factory-sealed.
* **The Solution:** Pack individual travel-sized packets (under 3.4 oz) or put full jars into checked luggage.

### 3. Cast Iron Skillets
Food lovers often purchase heavy heirloom cookware on vacation only to see it confiscated at security.
* **The Rule:** Cast iron cookware is prohibited in carry-on baggage because heavy, blunt metal objects are classified as potential impact bludgeoning weapons.
* **The Solution:** Checked luggage only. Standard thin aluminum pots and pans are generally permitted in carry-on, but cast iron is never allowed in the cabin.

### 4. Safety Razor Blades
While disposable plastic razors and cartridge razors (like Gillette or Venus) are completely allowed, safety razors trigger immediate confiscation.
* **The Rule:** Safety razors with removable double-edged blades are prohibited in carry-on unless the blade has been removed.
* **The Solution:** You can bring the safety razor handle in your carry-on, but the razor blades must travel in checked bags.

### 5. Cordless Curling Irons Powered by Butane Cartridges
Many modern cordless hair styling tools use small butane gas cartridges or butane fuel cells.
* **The Rule:** The FAA allows one cordless curling iron with a fitted safety cover per passenger in carry-on, but **spare gas cartridges are strictly forbidden** everywhere on the aircraft (both cabin and cargo hold).
* **The Solution:** Leave spare butane cartridges at home. Battery-operated rechargeable styling tools with lithium batteries must remain in carry-on bags.

### 6. Canned Soups, Tuna in Oil, and Salsa
Even if the can has more solid fish or vegetables than broth, if there is liquid inside that can slosh, the entire can is subject to the 3-1-1 liquids rule.
* **The Rule:** Any can larger than 3.4 oz cannot pass checkpoint screening.
* **The Solution:** Check your specialty gourmet canned goods or purchase them post-security.

### 7. Toy Guns, Nerf Blasters & Replica Weapons
Parents traveling with kids frequently get held up when miniature plastic blasters or squirt guns appear on the X-ray screen.
* **The Rule:** Under 49 CFR § 1540.111, **realistic replica firearms of any kind are strictly prohibited in carry-on**. Even brightly colored squirt guns are frequently confiscated if checkpoint supervisors determine they can cause panic or distraction.
* **The Solution:** Pack all toy blasters, water pistols, and foam dart guns in checked baggage.

### 8. Full-Size Toothpaste & Deodorant Pastes
You only used 20% of your 6-ounce toothpaste tube, so there is only 1.2 ounces left? TSA screeners do not measure remaining volume.
* **The Rule:** Checkpoint security enforces the **container volume**, not the liquid remaining inside. If the tube says "6.0 oz" on the label, it will be confiscated even if it is nearly empty.
* **The Solution:** Purchase designated travel-sized toothpaste tubes labeled 3.4 oz (100 ml) or smaller. Solid stick deodorants are exempt from the liquid rule.

### 9. Tent Stakes and Heavy Camping Mallets
Outdoor enthusiasts frequently attempt to fly with lightweight backpacking gear in their carry-on bags.
* **The Rule:** Metal [tent stakes](/items/tent-stakes/) are pointed objects that checkpoint screeners classify as sharp weapons.
* **The Solution:** Tent stakes and camping poles must be checked. The tent fabric itself is allowed in carry-on.

### 10. Tools Longer Than 7 Inches
Multi-tools, wrenches, and screwdrivers are common carry-on casualties.
* **The Rule:** Hand tools (like screwdrivers, wrenches, and pliers) exceeding **7 inches (18 cm)** in total length are prohibited in carry-on. Any tool with a knife blade of any length is prohibited.
* **The Solution:** Tools over 7 inches must be placed in checked luggage.

### 11. Flammable Liquid Lighters & Torch Lighters
While standard disposable Bic lighters are allowed in your pocket or carry-on (one per passenger), high-powered jet flame or blue-flame torch lighters are completely banned.
* **The Rule:** Torch lighters produce thin, intense, wind-resistant flames and are hazardous goods banned from both carry-on and checked luggage.
* **The Solution:** Leave torch lighters, plasma arc lighters without safety caps, and refillable fluid at home.

### 12. Oversized Salt Shakers, Spices & Protein Powders
Since 2018, the TSA enforces enhanced screening procedures for organic and inorganic powders.
* **The Rule:** Powders exceeding **12 ounces (350 ml / roughly the size of a soda can)** require separate inspection and are subject to confiscation if officers cannot verify their chemical composition.
* **The Solution:** Remove powder containers over 12 oz from your carry-on and place them in the inspection bin, or pack them in checked baggage.

## What Happens to Confiscated Items?

When an item is surrendered at a TSA checkpoint:
1. **Voluntary Abandonment:** Travelers sign over legal rights to the property upon surrendering it.
2. **Disposal vs Auction:** Perishable foods and open liquids are disposed of in hazmat or municipal waste bins. High-value confiscated items (tools, knives, corkscrews, jewelry) are transferred to state surplus agencies, which catalog and auction them in bulk lots to the public.
3. **Mailing Options:** Some major airports (such as SFO, ORD, and ATL) offer airport postal kiosks before the checkpoint where you can mail prohibited items home if you have sufficient time before boarding.
