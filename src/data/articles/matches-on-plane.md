Matches seem simple enough — but they're one of the more nuanced items in TSA regulations. The rules depend entirely on the **type of match** you're trying to bring. Safety matches and strike-anywhere matches are treated very differently by the TSA and FAA, and the carry-on vs. checked baggage rules may surprise you. This guide breaks it all down clearly.

---

## TSA Rules Summary

- **Safety Matches in Carry-On:** ✅ One book of safety matches allowed (on your person)
- **Safety Matches in Checked Baggage:** ❌ Not Allowed
- **Strike-Anywhere Matches in Carry-On:** ❌ Never allowed
- **Strike-Anywhere Matches in Checked Baggage:** ❌ Never allowed
- **TSA Declaration Required:** No (for permitted safety matches)

---

## Official Regulations

### The Key Difference: Safety Matches vs. Strike-Anywhere Matches

**Safety matches** (also called "book matches" or "safety strike matches") can only ignite when struck against the special phosphorus strip on the match packaging. They are the standard matches found in matchbooks, hotel matchboxes, and restaurant matches.

**Strike-anywhere matches** can ignite on any rough surface — the side of your shoe, a wall, sandpaper, etc. These are the classic "kitchen matches" or "farmer matches" — typically wooden with a red and white tip. They are significantly more dangerous for air travel.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Strike-anywhere matches are **prohibited on aircraft in both carry-on AND checked baggage** with no exceptions. This is an FAA regulation under 49 CFR 175.10.

### Safety Matches: The One-Book Rule

The TSA and FAA permit travelers to carry **one book of safety matches** on their person (not in luggage, but in a pocket or similar). The specific rules are:

- **One book only** — typically 20 matches
- Must be **safety matches** (not strike-anywhere)
- Must be **on your person** — not stored in carry-on bags, backpacks, or any luggage
- **Not permitted in checked baggage** — this surprises most travelers

Yes, you read that correctly: you CAN carry one safety matchbook on your person at security, but you CANNOT put matches in your checked luggage.

### Why Are Matches Banned from Checked Baggage?

This comes down to FAA fire safety regulations. In the cargo hold, a fire from accidentally ignited matches cannot be easily detected or suppressed. A small fire in an overhead bin can be addressed by crew; a fire in the cargo hold is catastrophic. This is why even "safe" safety matches are prohibited from checked baggage.

| Waterproof matches | ❌ Treat as strike-anywhere — prohibited | ❌ Prohibited |

## Lighters vs. Matches: What's Easier to Fly With?

Many travelers don't realize that **lighters are actually easier to bring on a plane** than matches:

- **Disposable lighters (Bic-style):** ✅ One lighter permitted in carry-on OR on your person
- **Torch lighters / jet lighters:** ❌ Not allowed
- **Lighter fluid:** ❌ Not allowed in any baggage

If you smoke or need fire for camping purposes, a standard disposable lighter is the most travel-friendly option.

## International Rules for Matches

Most countries follow similar ICAO (International Civil Aviation Organization) rules:

- **UK / Europe:** Same as TSA — one safety matchbook on your person, no checked bags
- **Australia:** Safety matches permitted in carry-on on your person
- **Singapore:** Matches are subject to airport authority discretion — check with your airline

Always confirm with your specific airline before traveling internationally with matches, as carrier policies can be stricter than minimum regulations.

## Essential Traveler Tips

1. **Keep matches in your shirt/jacket pocket** — Not your backpack, carry-on, or any bag that goes through the X-ray. On your person is the correct way to carry them through security.
2. **Consider a lighter instead** — A Bic-style disposable lighter is permitted in carry-on bags (not just on your person), making it far more convenient.
3. **For camping trips** — Buy matches or a lighter at your destination. Most outdoor stores, gas stations, and camping supply shops carry both.
4. **Check with your airline** — Even if TSA permits one book, your specific airline may prohibit matches entirely on international routes.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Mistake 1: Packing matches in checked luggage.** Many travelers assume "if I can't bring it in carry-on, I'll just check it." For matches, this logic fails — matches are prohibited in checked bags too.
- **Mistake 2: Bringing strike-anywhere matches thinking they're fine.** Strike-anywhere matches are not permitted anywhere on the aircraft. No exceptions.
- **Mistake 3: Putting a matchbook in your carry-on bag.** The one-book rule requires matches to be on your person — in a pocket — not inside any bag that goes through X-ray.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can you bring matches on a plane?**
A: Yes, with strict conditions. One book of safety matches is allowed on your person (in a pocket). Strike-anywhere matches are prohibited entirely.

**Q: Can you bring matches in carry-on luggage?**
A: No — even safety matches should be on your person (pocket), not inside a carry-on bag. However, in practice, a matchbook in a carry-on rarely gets flagged.

**Q: Can you put matches in checked baggage?**
A: No. All matches — including safety matches — are prohibited in checked baggage per FAA regulations.

**Q: Are matchbooks allowed on planes?**
A: One safety matchbook may be carried on your person. Matchbooks in carry-on or checked luggage are technically not permitted.

**Q: Can you take matches on a plane to the UK?**
A: Yes, UK rules mirror ICAO standards — one safety matchbook on your person is permitted.

**Q: Are waterproof matches allowed on planes?**
A: No. Waterproof matches are treated the same as strike-anywhere matches and are prohibited in all baggage.

## Conclusion

Matches are among the most restricted common items in aviation. Remember the two key rules: **only safety matches** are allowed (never strike-anywhere), and they must be carried **on your person** — not in any bag. Checked baggage is off-limits for all matches. If you need fire for your trip, a standard disposable lighter in your carry-on is actually a simpler and more flexible option. When in doubt, buy matches or a lighter at your destination.
