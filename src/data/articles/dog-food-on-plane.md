Traveling with a pet or heading to visit a dog and want to bring along their favorite food or treats? Navigating TSA rules for pet food can be confusing — especially because wet food (canned or pouched) and dry food are treated differently. This guide covers everything for dog food, cat food, dog treats, and pet snacks on domestic and international flights.

---

## TSA Rules Summary

- **Dry Dog/Cat Food in Carry-On:** ✅ Allowed
- **Wet/Canned Pet Food in Carry-On:** ⚠️ Restricted — 3.4 oz (100ml) or less per container
- **Dry Pet Food in Checked Baggage:** ✅ Allowed
- **Wet/Canned Pet Food in Checked Baggage:** ✅ Allowed
- **Dog Treats in Carry-On:** ✅ Generally Allowed (solid treats)
- **International Customs:** ⚠️ Significant restrictions apply

---

## Official Regulations

### Dry Dog Food and Cat Food

**Dry pet food is treated the same as dry human food by TSA** — it is a solid item and not subject to the 3-1-1 liquids rule. You can pack dry kibble in carry-on or checked baggage without quantity restrictions on domestic US flights.

Keep in mind:
- Dry food goes through the X-ray scanner without issue
- TSA officers may swab dry food for explosive residue screening
- Original sealed packaging is helpful but not required

### Wet/Canned Pet Food

Wet dog food, wet cat food, and food in pouches are **classified as liquids or gels** by the TSA — the same category as peanut butter or soup. This means:

- In **carry-on bags**: Maximum 3.4 oz (100ml) per container, must fit in your quart-sized liquid bag
- In **checked baggage**: No quantity limit

A standard can of wet dog food (5.5 oz to 13 oz) **cannot go in your carry-on bag** — it exceeds the liquid limit. Small travel-sized pouches under 3.4 oz are the only wet food option for carry-on.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The same rule applies to canned cat food, wet puppy food, and pet food pouches. If it's liquid, gel, or paste — it falls under the 3-1-1 rule.

### Dog Treats

Solid dog treats — biscuits, jerky sticks, chews, and similar — are solid food items and **allowed in carry-on and checked baggage** with no quantity restrictions on domestic flights. There is no TSA restriction on hard, solid pet treats.

Soft, moist dog treats (like soft training treats) may be scrutinized by TSA as they have a consistency closer to a gel. In practice they are usually waved through, but carry them in their original sealed packaging to make screening faster.

## Packing Pet Food for Carry-On vs. Checked Baggage

| Pet Food Type | Carry-On | Checked |
|---|---|---|
| Dry kibble (any brand) | ✅ Any amount | ✅ Any amount |
| Wet food in large can | ❌ Over liquid limit | ✅ Any amount |
| Wet food pouch (under 3.4 oz) | ✅ In liquid bag | ✅ Any amount |
| Hard dog treats | ✅ Any amount | ✅ Any amount |
| Soft moist treats | ✅ Usually fine | ✅ Any amount |
| Freeze-dried raw food | ✅ Solid — any amount | ✅ Any amount |

## International Flights: Pet Food Customs Rules

This is where it gets significantly more complicated. Customs authorities at international destinations often have **strict biosecurity rules on animal-based products**, which includes most pet food.

### Why International Pet Food Rules Are Strict

Pet food — especially meat-based products — can carry:
- Animal diseases (foot-and-mouth, African swine fever, etc.)
- Invasive pests
- Pathogens not present in the destination country

### Country-Specific Rules

**Australia:**
- Commercially packaged, sealed pet food *may* be permitted but must be declared
- Products containing certain meats (pork, beef) face extra scrutiny
- Fresh or raw pet food is generally prohibited
- Failure to declare = significant fines

**Canada:**
- Pet food for personal use is generally allowed from the US
- Must be declared on CBSA customs form
- Commercial quantities require permits

**European Union:**
- Pet food from non-EU countries subject to strict controls
- Commercially sealed, labeled food from approved countries more likely permitted
- Raw meat-based pet food generally prohibited

**Japan:**
- Commercially packaged pet food may be permitted
- Must declare all animal-based products
- Raw/fresh pet food subject to strict quarantine inspection

**United Kingdom:**
- Post-Brexit, EU pet food rules no longer automatically apply
- Must declare pet food and associated animal products
- Commercially sealed pet food from certain countries permitted

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The safest approach for international travel is to **not bring pet food from home**. Buy pet food at your destination from local pet stores. Most destinations stock international brands (Royal Canin, Hill's Science Diet, etc.).

## Essential Traveler Tips

1. **For short domestic trips** — Pack a resealable bag of dry kibble in your carry-on. No limits, no issues.
2. **For longer trips with a checked bag** — Pack full-size cans and bags of wet/dry food in your checked luggage.
3. **For international travel** — Don't bring pet food from home. Research and source the same or equivalent food at your destination.
4. **Keep original packaging** — For both TSA and customs, original sealed packaging with ingredient labels makes screening faster and reduces the chance of confiscation.
5. **Portion packs for travel** — Many brands sell single-serve packs and travel-sized pouches that are perfect for carry-on within the 3.4 oz limit.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Mistake 1: Packing a large can of wet dog food in your carry-on.** A standard 5.5 oz can is over the TSA liquid limit. It will be confiscated.
- **Mistake 2: Forgetting to declare pet food at international customs.** Even if you think it'll be allowed, always declare it. Non-declaration fines are significant worldwide.
- **Mistake 3: Bringing raw or fresh pet food internationally.** Raw meat products are strictly controlled at most international borders and will almost certainly be confiscated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can you bring dog food on a plane?**
A: Yes for domestic US flights — dry dog food in carry-on or checked bags, wet food in checked bags (or under 3.4 oz in carry-on).

**Q: Can you bring dog treats on a plane?**
A: Yes. Solid dog treats are allowed in carry-on and checked baggage on domestic US flights.

**Q: Can you bring dog treats through TSA?**
A: Yes. Hard, solid dog treats are not restricted by TSA at security checkpoints.

**Q: Can I bring wet cat food on a plane?**
A: In checked baggage, yes. In carry-on, only if each container is 3.4 oz (100ml) or less.

**Q: Can I bring dog food on an international flight?**
A: It depends entirely on the destination country's customs rules. Most countries require declaration and have restrictions on meat-based pet food. The safest option is to buy pet food at your destination.

**Q: Can you bring canned dog food in checked luggage?**
A: Yes. Canned pet food has no restrictions in checked luggage for US domestic flights.

---

## Conclusion

For domestic US travel, the rules are simple: dry pet food and solid treats are unrestricted in carry-on and checked baggage; wet or canned food must follow the 3-1-1 liquids rule in carry-on, but is unlimited in checked bags. For international travel, customs rules dominate — declare everything, avoid meat-based products where possible, and consider buying pet food at your destination to avoid complications. Your pet's food safety matters, but so does making it through customs without fines.
